13.8 Billion Years

Cosmic Timeline

From the Big Bang singularity to the present accelerating universe.

t ≈ 0 — 10⁻⁴³ s

Planck Epoch

All four fundamental forces unified. Quantum gravity dominates. Spacetime itself is foam-like and uncertain at the Planck scale (~10⁻³⁵ m).

t ≈ 10⁻³⁶ — 10⁻³² s

Cosmic Inflation

Exponential expansion stretches quantum fluctuations into the seeds of large-scale structure. The observable universe grows faster than light.

t ≈ 10⁻⁶ s — 3 min

Quark-Gluon → Nucleosynthesis

Quarks confine into protons and neutrons. Big Bang nucleosynthesis forges hydrogen, helium, and traces of lithium — the primordial abundance still observed today.

t ≈ 380,000 years

Recombination & CMB

Electrons bind to nuclei. The universe becomes transparent. The Cosmic Microwave Background is released — a snapshot of the infant cosmos at z ≈ 1100.

t ≈ 200 Myr — 1 Gyr

First Stars & Galaxies

Dark matter halos collapse. Population III stars ignite, reionize the intergalactic medium, and begin enriching the cosmos with heavier elements.

t ≈ 9.8 Gyr → Now

Dark Energy Dominance

Cosmic acceleration begins. Dark energy (or a cosmological constant Λ) overtakes matter density, driving the universe toward exponential expansion once more.

Invisible Gravity

Dark Matter Explorer

~27% of the universe’s energy density. It does not emit, absorb, or reflect light — yet it shapes galaxies.

🌌

Gravitational Evidence

Galaxy rotation curves remain flat at large radii. Visible matter alone cannot provide enough centripetal force. Dark matter halos supply the missing mass.

🔭

Gravitational Lensing

Mass bends light. Strong and weak lensing maps reveal mass distributions that far exceed luminous matter — especially in galaxy clusters (Bullet Cluster).

🧬

Candidates

WIMPs, axions, sterile neutrinos, primordial black holes… None yet detected in the lab. The simulation treats dark matter as collisionless, heavier particles.

📊

In This Simulation

Toggle “Show Dark Matter” to reveal the invisible component. Increase the ratio to watch how structure formation accelerates under extra gravitational influence.

Microscopic Universe

Quantum Realm

Uncertainty, discrete jumps, and vacuum fluctuations — the rules that govern the smallest scales.

Uncertainty & Quantum Noise

Heisenberg’s principle forbids simultaneous precise knowledge of position and momentum. In the simulation, Quantum Mode injects stochastic kicks and occasional tunneling-like jumps.

  • Random velocity perturbations proportional to noise strength
  • Rare discontinuous position jumps (tunneling analogy)
  • Visual “quantum flashes” when particles receive strong kicks
  • Illustrates how microscopic randomness seeds macroscopic structure
General Relativity

Spacetime Laboratory

Mass tells spacetime how to curve; spacetime tells mass how to move.

Curvature & Black Holes

The grid warps around black holes. Particles that cross the event horizon are absorbed. An orange accretion disk and yellow photon ring mark the strong-field region.

  • Event horizon: point of no return (simplified Schwarzschild-like)
  • Photon ring: unstable photon orbits (visual only)
  • Grid distortion proportional to local gravitational potential
  • Click anywhere on the main canvas to spawn a new black hole

Standard Model

12 fermions (quarks + leptons), 4 force carriers (γ, g, W, Z), and the Higgs boson. Gravity remains outside the quantum framework.

🕳️

Black Hole Physics

Schwarzschild radius rₛ = 2GM/c². Information paradox, Hawking radiation, and the holographic principle remain active research frontiers.

Hierarchy of Scales

From 10⁻³⁵ m (Planck) to 10²⁶ m (observable universe) — 61 orders of magnitude. This simulation compresses that drama into a canvas.
